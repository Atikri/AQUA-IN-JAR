006: Formants & Vowel Tuning

January 25, 2026

← Back to Master Map

📅 Session 6: Formants (The Acoustic EQ)#

Textbook: Master Handbook of Acoustics (Everest) or Seikel (Chapter on Resonation).

Now we combine Biology (Module 3) with Physics (Module 5). This is the “Secret Code” of singing.

1. What is a Formant?#

The vocal tract is an air-filled tube. Like a bottle, it has a “natural resonance frequency” where air likes to vibrate. Because the tube is complex (throat + mouth), it has multiple resonance peaks. We call these peaks Formants.

  • Source: Produces Harmonics (H1, H2, H3…).
  • Filter: Has Formants (F1, F2, F3…).
  • The Interaction: When a Harmonic passes through a Formant, it gets BOOSTED (turns loud). When it falls between Formants, it gets DAMPENED (turns quiet).

2. The Vowel Code (F1 & F2)#

The ear does not hear “Jaw position”; the ear hears frequencies. Specifically, the ear identifies vowels based on the spacing between F1 and F2.

Formant 1 (F1) = Mouth Opening / Throat Height#

  • Rule: F1 is controlled by how open the jaw/throat is.
  • Low F1 (Close to H1): Closed vowels (EE, OO). Small mouth opening.
  • High F1: Open vowels (AH). Big mouth opening.
  • Practical: If you want to boost the sound (Loudness), drop your jaw to raise F1.

Formant 2 (F2) = Tongue Shape (Front vs Back)#

  • Rule: F2 is controlled by the tongue body position.
  • High F2: Front Vowels (EE, EH). Tongue is forward.
  • Low F2: Back Vowels (OO, OH). Tongue is back.

3. Practical Magic: Formant Tuning#

Opera singers and Belters use this strategy. The Problem: If you sing a high pitch (Source) but keep your mouth closed (Low F1 Filter), the Harmonics might miss the Formant completely. The voice will crack or strain. The Solution (Tuning): As you sing higher, you must OPEN your mouth (Raise F1) to “chase” the rising pitch.

  • Example: A Soprano singing a High C cannot sing the vowel “EE”. She must modify it toward “AH” to tune the Formant to the Pitch.

📝 Session 6 Quiz (Completed)#

Q1: See the Sound#

If I look at a Spectrogram, I see horizontal “bands” of darkness where the sound is loudest. What are those dark bands called?

Answer: Formants. These dark bands are the “Open Doors” where harmonics are being boosted by the throat shape.

Q2: The Jaw Rule#

I am singing the vowel “EE” (Closed). Does this vowel have a naturally Low F1 or High F1?

Answer: Low F1. Closed mouth/jaw = Low Formant 1 frequency.

Q3: The Modification#

Why do singers “drop their jaw” when they sing high notes? (In terms of physics)

Answer: To raise F1 (and boost loudness). As the pitch (F0) rises, the singer must raise the filter (F1) to “catch” the pitch. If they kept the jaw closed (Low F1), the high pitch would hit a “wall” and get dampened. Dropping the jaw aligns the “Open Door” (Formant) with the Note (Harmonic).
