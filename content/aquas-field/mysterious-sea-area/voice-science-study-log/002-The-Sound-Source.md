## 📅 Session 2: The Sound Source (The Larynx)
**Textbook**: *Anatomy Physiology for Speech, Language, and Hearing (Seikel et al.)*

We have built the chassis (ribs) and filled the tank (lungs). Now we install the **Engine**: The **Larynx** (Voice Box).

### 1. The Structure: The Cartilage Framework
The larynx is a tube of cartilage sitting on top of the trachea (windpipe). It is suspended from a floating bone called the **Hyoid Bone**.

*   **The Foundation**: The **Cricoid Cartilage**. A complete ring (shaped like a signet ring, taller in the back) that sits on top of the trachea.
*   **The Shield**: The **Thyroid Cartilage**. The large, V-shaped shield that protects the front. The "Adam's Apple" is the notch of this shield. It rocks forward and backward on the Cricoid.
*   **The Controllers**: The **Arytenoid Cartilages**. Two tiny pyramids sitting on the back rim of the Cricoid. **Crucial**: The vocal folds are attached to these. When the Arytenoids move, the vocal folds move.

### 2. The Intrinsice Muscles (The Crew)
These muscles are inside the larynx and control the vocal folds directly. We categorize them by *function*:

*   **Abductors (The Openers)**:
    *   **PCA (Posterior Cricoarytenoid)**: The **ONLY** muscle that opens the glottis. It pulls the Arytenoids outward. Used for inhalation.
*   **Adductors (The Closers)**:
    *   **LCA (Lateral Cricoarytenoid)**: Rotates Arytenoids inward to close the cords.
    *   **IA (Interarytenoids)**: Slides Arytenoids together to seal the back gap.
*   **Tensors (The Pitch Changers)**:
    *   **CT (Cricothyroid)**: Rocks the Thyroid forward, stretching the vocal folds. **Higher Pitch**.
    *   **TA (Thyroarytenoid/Vocalis)**: The body of the vocal fold itself. When it contracts, it shortens and thickens. **Lower Pitch / Louder Chest Voice**.

### 3. The Physics: How Sound Happens
The vocal folds do not "clap" together like hands. They vibrate in the airstream.
**The Myoelastic-Aerodynamic Theory**:
1.  **Adduction**: Muscles bring folds together.
2.  **Pressure**: Air from lungs builds up below (Subglottal Pressure).
3.  **Blast**: Air blows them apart.
4.  **Recoil + Bernoulli**: The elasticity of the tissue + the **Bernoulli Effect** (drop in pressure caused by high speed air) sucks them back together.
5.  *Repeat 100-1000 times per second.*
