## 📅 Session 10: Therapy & Repair (The Clinic)
**Textbook**: *Clinical Voice Pathology (Stemple)* or *The Vocal Athlete*.

The car has crashed. Now we enter the repair shop.
This module teaches you how to recognize injury and how to heal it.

### 1. The Big Three Injuries
*   **Nodules ("Calluses")**:
    *   *Cause*: Long-term chronic abuse (months/years).
    *   *Appearance*: Hard calluses on BOTH sides (bilateral) directly opposite each other.
    *   *Treatment*: Voice Therapy (99% success). Surgery rarely needed.
*   **Polyps ("Blisters")**:
    *   *Cause*: Often a SINGLE traumatic event (one screaming match).
    *   *Appearance*: A fluid-filled sac on ONE side (unilateral).
    *   *Treatment*: Surgery usually required, followed by therapy.
*   **Hemorrhage ("Bruise")**:
    *   *Cause*: Burst blood vessel (singing while sick/on aspirin).
    *   *Appearance*: Red blood spreading under the tissue.
    *   *Treatment*: **COMPLETE VOCAL REST** (7-14 days). No speaking, no whispering.

### 2. The Healing Tool: SOVT Exercises
**Semi-Occluded Vocal Tract (SOVT)** is the gold standard for therapy.
*   *Concept*: By partially blocking the mouth (using a straw or lip trill), you create "Back Pressure."
*   *Result*: The air pressure pushes the vocal folds apart slightly, allowing them to vibrate with **less collision force**. It massages them from the inside out.
*   *Exercise*: Straw Phonation. Hum through a small stirring straw.

### 3. Red Flags (When to see a Doctor)
If you have hoarseness that lasts longer than **2 weeks** without illness, you MUST see an ENT (Laryngologist).
*   *Myth*: "I can just whisper to save my voice."
*   *Fact*: Whispering is **worse** than speaking because it forces the posterior arytenoids to squeeze tight.

### 📝 Final Quiz (Session 10) (Completed)

#### Q1: The Diagnosis
**I look at your vocal cords. I see hard, white bumps on BOTH sides in the exact same spot. Are these likely Nodules or Polyps?**

> **Answer: Nodules.**
> Nodules are bilateral (both sides) because they are contact calluses caused by the cords hitting each other over time.

#### Q2: The Remedy
**You have a Vocal Hemorrhage (burst blood vessel). Should you practice gentle exercises or do Complete Vocal Rest?**

> **Answer: Complete Vocal Rest.**
> Absolutely no sound. The blood needs to reabsorb. If you vibrate the tissue while it is bruised, you can cause permanent scarring (Fibrosis).

#### Q3: The Physics of Healing
**Why does humming through a small straw help heal a tired voice?**

> **Answer: Back Pressure (SOVT).**
> The constriction creates air pressure that "cushions" the vocal folds. This pushes them slightly apart, reducing collision force while still engaging the muscles—like a "massage from the inside out."
