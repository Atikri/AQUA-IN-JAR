# Unit 2: Judgment & Weight
**Roots**: *Crim* (Fault/Crime), *Prob* (Prove/Honest), *Grav* (Heavy), *Lev* (Light)

This unit contrasts the concepts of judgment (Crime vs. Proof) and physical weight (Heaviness vs. Lightness).

---

## 1. CRIM
**Origin**: Latin *cernere* ("to decide") -> *crimen* ("verdict" or "accusation").
*(Related to: Crime, Critic)*

### **Criminology**
*(noun)* The scientific study of crime, criminals, and punishment.
> *She majored in **criminology** hoping to understand the psychological roots of theft.*

**Deep Dive**:
Criminology is not just police work; it combines sociology, psychology, and law. A *criminologist* studies the *trends* and *causes* of crime rather than solving individual cases (which is what a detective does).

### **Decriminalize**
*(verb)* To remove or reduce the criminal status of an act.
> *Many states have moved to **decriminalize** minor traffic offenses.*

**Deep Dive**:
This is distinct from "legalize". If you *legalize* something, it becomes fully allowed (like selling apples). If you *decriminalize* something (like jaywalking or certain minor drug offenses), it is still against the rules, but you won't go to jail for it—you might just get a fine. It lowers the stakes from a "crime" to a "civil infraction".

### **Incriminate**
*(verb)* To show evidence of involvement in a crime or fault.
> *The emails were found to **incriminate** the CEO in the fraud scheme.*

**Deep Dive**:
To *incriminate* is to "put the crime in" someone. It means to provide proof of guilt. The Fifth Amendment gives you the right to "avoid self-incrimination"—meaning you don't have to provide evidence against yourself.

### **Recrimination**
*(noun)* An accusation in response to one from someone else.
> *The meeting dissolved into bitter **recrimination**, with everyone blaming each other for the failure.*

**Deep Dive**:
A *recrimination* is a counter-attack. It is less about justice and more about defense ("I may be wrong, but you are worse!"). It combines *re-* (back) + *crimen* (accusation). It often appears in the plural: "bitter recriminations."

## 2. PROB
**Origin**: Latin *probare* meaning "to prove" or "to test".
*(Related to: Probe, Probation)*

### **Approbation**
*(noun)* Formal or official approval; praise.
> *The new policy received the **approbation** of the board of directors.*

**Deep Dive**:
*Approbation* is a high-level word for approval. It suggests a formal "stamp of approval" from a superior or a group. The opposite is *disapprobation* (moral disapproval). It is close to the word "praise" but feels heavier and more official.

### **Probate**
*(noun)* The process of proving in court that the will of someone who has died is valid.
> *The estate was stuck in **probate** for months before the heirs received anything.*

**Deep Dive**:
When someone dies, their Will must be "proved" genuine. This legal process is called *probate*. Until a court grants probate, the assets are frozen. It literally means "the proof."

### **Probity**
*(noun)* Absolute honesty and uprightness.
> *The judge was known for his **probity**; no one ever questioned his integrity.*

**Deep Dive**:
A person of *probity* has been "tested" and found good. It is a very strong synonym for integrity. While "honesty" means telling the truth, "probity" implies an incorruptible character that stands up to scrutiny.

### **Reprobate**
*(noun)* A person of thoroughly bad character.
> *His uncle was a lovable old **reprobate** who told wild stories and gambled too much.*

**Deep Dive**:
A *reprobate* is someone who has "failed the test" of morality. Originally, it meant someone rejected by God (a sinner destined for hell). Today, it is often used humorously or affectionately to describe a rascal or a scoundrel, though it can still describe a truly wicked person.

## 3. GRAV
**Origin**: Latin *gravis* meaning "heavy, weighty, serious".
*(Related to: Gravity, Grief)*

### **Grave**
*(adj)* Requiring serious thought or concern.
> *The doctor had a **grave** expression as he delivered the diagnosis.*

**Deep Dive**:
While also a noun (a place of burial), the adjective *grave* means "heavy with importance." A "grave mistake" is one with serious consequences. To look "grave" provides a visual of the weight of the situation pulling one's face down.

### **Gravitas**
*(noun)* Great or very dignified seriousness.
> *The young actor lacked the **gravitas** needed to play the role of the aging king.*

**Deep Dive**:
*Gravitas* is the quality of having "weight" in a room. It is a presence that commands respect. Politicians, news anchors, and leaders cultivate gravitas. It implies stability, seriousness, and maturity.

### **Gravitate**
*(verb)* To move or be drawn toward something, especially by natural tendency.
> *Students rarely **gravitate** toward studying on Friday nights.*

**Deep Dive**:
Just as physical gravity pulls objects to the center, we metaphorically *gravitate* toward things we like or are naturally suited for. It implies an unconscious or natural flow, rather than a forced decision.

### **Aggravate**
*(verb)* [1] To make (an injury, problem) worse. [2] To annoy or bother.
> *Running on a sprained ankle will only **aggravate** the injury.*

**Deep Dive**:
Literally "to make heavier" (ad + gravis). Strict grammarians argue that *aggravate* should only mean "to worsen a condition" (definition 1). However, in common usage, it almost always means "to annoy" (definition 2). If you want to be precise, use *exacerbate* for "worsen" and *irritate* for "annoy," but *aggravate* works for both.

## 4. LEV
**Origin**: Latin *levis* meaning "light" (in weight).
*(Related to: Lever, Leaven)*

### **Alleviate**
*(verb)* To make (suffering, deficiency, or a problem) less severe.
> *Aspirin helps to **alleviate** the pain of a headache.*

**Deep Dive**:
To *alleviate* is to "lighten the load." You rarely cure or fix a problem completely when you alleviate it; you just make it bearable. You alleviate poverty, pain, or traffic.

### **Elevation**
*(noun)* The height of a place; the act of lifting up.
> *The **elevation** of the mountain peak made breathing difficult.*

**Deep Dive**:
This is simply the measurement of how "lifted" something is. It can refer to physical height (above sea level) or social enhancement (elevation to the peerage).

### **Cantilever**
*(noun)* A long piece of wood or metal that sticks out from a wall to support something above it.
> *The balcony was supported by a steel **cantilever**.*

**Deep Dive**:
This technical architectural term refers to a beam that is supported only on one end. It relies on the physics of leverage (*lev*) to hold up weight. Think of a diving board: it is a cantilever.

### **Levity**
*(noun)* Humor or lack of seriousness, especially when inappropriate.
> *His attempt at **levity** during the funeral was met with stony silence.*

**Deep Dive**:
*Levity* is "lightness" of spirit. It is the direct opposite of *gravity*. While "humor" is usually neutral or good, *levity* often implies that the situation *should* be serious, but the person is treating it lightly. It implies a lack of respect or appropriate weight.
