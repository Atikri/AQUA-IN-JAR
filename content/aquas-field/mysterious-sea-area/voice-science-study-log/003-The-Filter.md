## 📅 Session 3: The Filter (Resonance)
**Textbook**: *Anatomy Physiology for Speech, Language, and Hearing (Seikel et al.)* -- Chapters on Articulatory/Resonatory System.

We have the **Power** (Lungs) and the **Source** (Larynx). But the raw sound is just a buzz. Now we sculpt it into something beautiful.

### 1. The Concept: Source-Filter Theory
This is the "E=mc²" of Voice Science. Proposed by Gunnar Fant.
*   **Source (Larynx)**: Creates the raw signal. Determines **Pitch (F0)** and **Harmonics**.
    *   *Analogy*: The guitar string vibrating.
*   **Filter (Vocal Tract)**: Shapes the signal. Determines **Vowels** and **Timbre**.
    *   *Analogy*: The body of the guitar amplifying specific frequencies.
*   **Independence**: You can sing the same pitch (Source) on different vowels (Filter). You can sing the same vowel (Filter) on different pitches (Source).

### 2. The Resonators (The Echo Chambers)
The "Vocal Tract" is a series of air-filled tubes above the larynx.
*   **Laryngopharynx**: The bottom tube, right above the vocal cords.
*   **Oropharynx**: The back of the mouth/throat.
*   **Nasopharynx**: The space behind the nose (above the soft palate).
*   **Oral Cavity**: The mouth itself.

### 3. The Articulators (The Movers)
To change the shape of the tube (and thus the vowel), we move things:
*   **Mandible (Jaw)**: The major opener.
*   **Tongue**: The MVP. It has two parts:
    *   *Body*: Arches high (for 'EE') or lays low (for 'AH').
    *   *Tip*: touches teeth for consonants (T, D, N).
*   **Velum (Soft Palate)**: The Door to the Nose.
    *   *Raised*: Closes off the nose (Oral sounds: A, E, I, O, U, B, P, K).
    *   *Lowered*: Opens the nose (Nasal sounds: M, N, NG).

### ⚡ Practical Application: The "Singer's Formant" (The 3000Hz Ring)
Why can an opera singer be heard over an orchestra without a microphone?
They cluster their throat resonators (Epilarynx) in a specific 1:6 ratio to boost a frequency band around **3000 Hz**. This is the "Ring" or "Squillo" in the voice.
**How to do it?** The "Twang". Narrowing the Epilarynx tube (Aryepiglottic Sphincter) creates this boost. It feels like a "Nasty Witch" or "Duck" sound, but when combined with a warm mouth shape, it projects power without strain.

### 📝 Session 3 Quiz (Completed)

#### Q1: Source vs. Filter
**If I change from singing "AH" to "EE" while staying on the same note, what have I changed?**
*(The Source or The Filter?)*

> **Answer: The Filter.**
> The pitch (Source) stayed the same. The shape of the tube (Filter) changed.
> *(User Note: "Tongue's position" is also correct, as the tongue IS the filter change).*

#### Q2: The Nasal Door
**To sing a clear, non-nasal vowel (like "AH"), what must the Soft Palate (Velum) do?**
*(Raise/Close or Lower/Open?)*

> **Answer: Raise / Close (Velum UP)**.
> When the Velum goes UP, it seals off the nasal cavity. Sound must exit the mouth. If it lowers, sound leaks into the nose (forming M/N/NG).

#### Q3: The MVP
**Which muscle is responsible for creating distinct vowels by arching high or lying flat to change the shape of the Oral Cavity?**

> **Answer: The Tongue.**
> It is the primary articulator.
