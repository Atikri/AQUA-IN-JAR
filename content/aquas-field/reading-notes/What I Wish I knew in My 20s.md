在分享这篇文章之前，我一定要说一句，在听这段播客的时候，印象最深、感触最深的一句话就是：如果你在 20 多岁感觉很糟糕，就说明你走在了正确的轨道上。如果你 20 岁过得很好，一帆风顺的话，那么可能会有一些糟糕的事情在未来发生。  
所以，如果和我一样在20多岁追梦，但是迷茫，孤单，不要灰心，至少有一件事我们值得庆祝，我们走在了20岁正确的轨道上。20多岁就是要经历一些糟糕，坚持下去，保持自我独特性，持续耕耘~希望今天这一期blog可以帮助到你✊  
> Your twenties are probably not going to be the best years of your life, at least not emotionally. While these may or may not be the years when you look your best, they are unlikely to be the years when you feel your best. In fact, statistically speaking, your twenties are likely to be the years when you will feel your worst.[^1]  

> I have to tell you, I have been doing this for 25 years. I have never heard from an old student or client who said, "Gosh, I wish I was back in my twenties."[^2] 

> They all reach out and say life is so much better in their thirties or in their forties. You know, the twenties were the worst decade of my life.[^3]   

> That's totally normal. That's a sign that you are doing your 20s correctly. You are on track.[^4]

很多人会告诉你：“20多岁是你一生中最美好的时光。”但事实上，从实证角度看，这通常是==成年期最艰难、最不确定的十年==。在这个阶段，你面临着职场、恋爱、财务以及自我认知的多重初试（Firsts）与挫折（Worsts）。  
临床心理学家 Dr. Meg Jay 认为，20多岁绝非可以挥霍的“青春期延长版”，而是“**定义性十年**”。
> The truth is, your twenties are the most defining decade of adulthood and also, in many ways, the most difficult decade in adulthood. The reason for that is it's a time of a lot of firsts and a lot of worsts.[^5]

## Dr. Meg Jay介绍
Dr. Meg Jay拥有加州大学伯克利分校（UC Berkeley）的临床心理学博士学位。目前，她是弗吉尼亚大学（University of Virginia, UVA）的教授。  
• 临床经验：在临床实践中拥有超过 20 年的经验，专门致力于研究和辅导 20 多岁的年轻群体。  
• **畅销书作家**：  
    ◦ 她撰写了具有代际影响力的畅销书[《The Defining Decade》](https://www.amazon.co.jp/-/en/Defining-Decade-Your-Twenties-Matter/dp/0446561754)，探讨了为什么 20 多岁是人生最重要的十年以及如何度过这段时光。  
    ◦ 她还出版了新书[《The 20something Treatment》](https://www.amazon.co.jp/-/en/Twentysomething-Treatment-Meg-Jay/dp/0008607621)，旨在为这个充满不确定性的年龄段提供革命性的解决方案。  
• **公众影响力**：  
    ◦ 她的 TED 演讲点击量已超过 1700 万次。  
    ◦《纽约时报》将她誉为“二十多岁年轻人的守护神”（patron saint for 20something）。

## 为什么20岁至关重要？
我们常听人说“30岁才是新的20岁”，但这其实是一个危险的谎言。Dr. Meg Jay 指出了几个关键事实：  
• 80% 的人生定义时刻发生在 **35 岁**之前。  
• 收入增长曲线在**工作的头十年**基本奠定。  
> Your learning curve in your 20s predicts your earning curve in your 30s, 40s, and 50s. [^6] 

• 大脑与人格的塑造在**20多岁时**比生命中任何其他时期都要剧烈。  

> Put yourself on that steep learning curve because the learning curve slows down after your 20s, and you kind of have less to take to the market. Seek out those learning opportunities.[^7]  

• 社交网络的规模在此时达到顶峰，之后会逐渐缩小。  

核心挑战：不确定的恐慌 20多岁最难的地方在于，一切都是==不确定==的。你不知道自己会在哪里工作，不知道会和谁结婚，甚至不知道能不能付得起房租。大脑倾向于将这种“不确定性”解读为“危险”，导致年轻人普遍处于一种==慢性焦虑状态==。  
> So, 20-somethings are walking around in a pretty low-level state of chronic stress and anxiety. Everything feels dangerous because they don't have those adult sources of safety.[^8]  

---

年轻人其实是最孤单的人群，某些朋友突然断绝联系，在工作上没有进展，失去了自己的兴趣等等。从经验上来说，20 岁的确就是人生中==最孤独的阶段==。  

> Empirically, your 20s tend to be the loneliest years of life.[^9]

## 职场破局：积累你的“身份资本”
如果你现在正处于一份不尽如人意的职业中，或者感到“大材小用”（Underemployed），请警惕：研究显示，75% 的低度就业者在==十年后依然处于这种状态==。
> It is actually pretty dangerous to note that 35% of 20-somethings who are underemployed[^10] are still underemployed a decade later.[^11]   

**解决方案**：==投资身份资本==（Identity Capital） 身份资本是指那些能==增加你个人价值==的投资。  
> You build it with a good piece of identity capital at a time. Identity capital is actually a really fabulous and flexible concept.[^12]  


• 不要只看薪水，要看学习曲线：20多岁的学习曲线决定了你未来几十年的收入曲线。即使是一份普通的 barista 工作，如果你能在业余时间考取证书、领导社群，这也是在**积累资本**。  
> Going to class is just showing up for life; it gets you out of bed and, like we were talking about with work, it adds structure to your day. So, just going to class is some very low-hanging fruit, but it is an amazing way to start.[^13]   

> Another piece of advice is to make a calendar. Take all your syllabi, and put them all in one place.[^14]  

> The best preparation for work is work.(have an internship.)[^15]  

> On average, young adults will have 9 different jobs by the age of 35.[^16]  

> We could have never foreseen the outcome, but it is just about getting out there and doing things that add value and are investments in yourself. You should focus on building skills that you can take into the workplace, which lead to other skills and eventually to other opportunities.[^17]  

> The great thing about identity capital is it can be a lot of things, depending on who you are trying to be or who you think you might be trying to be.[^18]  

> Young adults often have thoughts and experience, but they haven't **articulated** that to themselves yet. That is part of what we do together.[^19]    

> Here are four questions to ask yourself when you are feeling like you have no idea:  
> 1. What are you good at?  
> 2. What do you enjoy?  
> 3. What might pay your bills?  
        And maybe not immediately, but one day, what's going to give me the lifestyle that I think I want or need?
> 4. What does the world need?[^20]  

> Not to have too black-and-white a view on whether this immediately uses my degree as much as what identity capital is in this for me. Can I turn this into the next thing?[^21]  

• 变“愿望清单”为“行动计划”：不要说“**以后会解决的**”，因为解决问题本身也需要时间。[^22][^23]   
> Instead of saying, "Oh, it's fine; it doesn't matter; you will be fine," I say, "Hey, I'm taking your concerns seriously. So, what are we going to do? What are you going to do? Where can you start? Let's start now." That actually makes people feel better than telling them they will be fine.[^24]

## 亲密关系：是“主动决定”还是“被动滑入”？
• 如果你在 20 多岁时，没有在经营亲密关系这方面有一定的经验，那么就有可能会损失一些机会。此外，如果在 20 多岁一直保持单身的话，那么在接近 30 岁的时候，==自尊心==会有明显的下降。  
> Being chronically uncoupled may be especially detrimental to men, as those who remained single throughout their twenties experienced a significant dip in their self-esteem near 30.[^25]  

• 我们谈过，在 35 岁之前可能会换 9 份工作；在亲密关系中，或许==至少也会有多于一段的亲密关系==。   
> I don't know if you will have nine different relationships, but you will probably have more than one.[^26]   

• 当你感觉在这段亲密关系中非常被动，或者已经不想继续经营下去，而亲密关系本身也不再起作用的时候，如果我们想要离开，就应该==尽早离开==。  
> When you see something isn't working, it's time to move on.[^27]   
> If you are already planning, looking forward to, or anticipating needing a different partner later, we just need to do that now.[^28] 

• 很多人在 20 多岁想寻找到的问题答案，都是 “这个人适合我吗？” 但这其实没有答案，这==只是一个选择==。
所以你真正应该要看到的是：我和这个人的契合度有多高、我们==怎么样去解决我们之间的差异，以及那种不契合的感觉等等==。以及要知道，我们应该要放手，让人们和我们自己之间有不同点，这样才是非常有趣的。

• 你选择的伴侣是你一生中最重要的决定，因为这关乎你不仅选择了另一半，还**选择了你未来的“新家庭”**。  
> When you pick your partner, you are picking your new family.[^29]   

> You can't pick your family, but you can pick your friends. It's especially important for those 20-somethings who grew up with unhappy families.[^30]  

警惕“**滑入式关系**”（Sliding vs. Deciding） 很多伴侣因为经常在一起过夜，就“顺便”同居了；因为同居了三年，就“顺便”结婚了。这种==缺乏主观意志==的“滑入”往往是日后离婚的伏笔。  
可以问自己一些问题：  
> What if I am still in this situation five years from now?[^31]   
> What if I am still in this relationship five years from now?[^32]  
> As I asked that question, what are the feelings that you are having?[^33]

• 进行“29场对话”：在承诺前，必须弄清楚双方对金钱、宗教、孩子、家务和政治的看法。  
我觉得如果是情侣日常想要维护感情，用这些部分的问题进行深入探讨，互相感恩也是非常好的~这边试举书中的几个例子：  
> Do I make you a better person?[^34]   
> How are we going to manage our money?[^35]   
> Do you want to have kids?[^36]   
> What kind of parents do we want to be?[^37]   
> How traditional are you when it comes to gender roles?[^38]   
> How do you like to keep a house?[^39]   
> How will we keep our relationship alive over the long run?[^40]  
> What does your future look like?[^41]  
> Why do you like me?[^42]  
> Why do I like myself?[^43]  
> How important is travel to you?[^44]  
> How do you spend your free time?[^45]  
> Have you ever cheated on a partner? If so, when and why? Do you think it's possible you would cheat on me?[^46]  
> What makes you feel loved?[^47]  
> What do you need after a tough day?[^48]  
> 🎯If the answer is "I don't know" or "not sure", then start here.[^49]  

• 重塑你的“**被需求感**”：不要基于高中时那些“不成熟的反馈”来定义自己的魅力。==你只需要找到一个合适的匹配者，而不是让所有人满意==。  

> Their perceived desirability is based on what happened with counting heads in high school. They are going off some scans and bad data, which they then take into college or their 20s. They spend 10 years making dating and life decisions based on whether the boys in high school or the people in college wanted to be with them or not.[^50]  

> A big part of my work with young adults—or if you are listening in your 20s—==is a bit of a course correction for you==. ==It involves shifting those conversations you are having in your head about that data and about your perceived desirability==.[^51]   

> What else can you be telling yourself besides "it's hopeless," "you are powerless," or "there's nothing that you can do"?[^52]  

> The really wonderful thing about finding a partner is that you just need one good one. You just need one match.[^53]   

此外，Dr. Meg Jay 鼓励有野心的女性：你可以同时对事业和家庭有追求，生孩子并不是结束，而是一个更有动力、更高效的新开始。  
> I think the thing to understand is that having a baby is not the end; it's the beginning.[^54]

## 心理重建：从“万一”到“还有什么？”
WHAT IF ➡️ WHAT ELSE  
20多岁的社交焦虑本质上是“**社交不确定性**(Social uncertainty)”。你担心的不是自己不够好，而是不知道别人是否喜欢你。  
• 技能胜过药物（Skills over Pills）：虽然药物可以缓解情绪，但它不能教你如何与老板沟通、如何处理冲突。  
• ***经验**是最好的疗法，**生活**是最好的治疗师，因此，很焦虑的人们应该把自己放在社会情景中，从你信任的人开始，去展现自己的实力，慢慢培养起确定性。当我们的关系更加扩大、坚固，有了自己的团队和伙伴的时候，我们就建立起了社会社交确定性。而扩大关系不仅仅只是和同学坐在教室里面，而是真正地去**发展一系列的新技能**。要和新的人进行==更多的交流==，正是通过这种方式，我们学到了新的工作，有了新的机会。  
> The worst thing you can do is avoid and just stay in your head about that. The best thing you can do is put yourself in social situations that go well.[^55]  

> To build social certainty and confidence:
> 1. Start with people you trust[^56]  
> 2. Make sure you are showing up for your job[^57]  
> 3. Ensure you are having those difficult conversations[^58]  

> By doing these things, you will start to have a little bit more social certainty or more social confidence.[^59]  

> And remember that most people are not socially confident until the end of their twenties or beyond.[^60]  

> Talking to new people when you move to a new town and sign up for a walking club or a running club is how you make new friends. This is how you talk to someone you think you might like in a bar or at a conference.[^61]  

> Being willing to talk to people that you don't already feel comfortable with and maybe they are different, older, in positions of power, or just new. That is the single most important skill. It branches out and sets you into all those different areas.[^62]   

• 思维转换：当你陷入“万一（What if）生活搞砸了”的恐惧时，试着问自己：“==还有什么（What else）可能发生==？”。这种思维能帮你从瘫痪的焦虑中跳出来，进入解决问题的模式。

## 勇敢地想象生活会变好
最好的疗法就是==希望==。生活会在 30 岁、40 岁、50 岁的时候慢慢变好。  
所以我们==要有自信==，并且相信我们还会走很长的一段路。我们会在这段路中得到经验，帮助我们成长。  
> Hope is the biggest predictor of future happiness.[^63]   

> I have a belief that things are going to work out, and I think that helps people.[^64]

无论是对于我们自己，或者是当我们想要教育年轻人和小朋友的时候，都可以给他们一些东西引导他们进行沉思。比如：给他们一本书让他们去阅读，或者发送一个播客让他们去听。我们的目标是至少开始在他们或自己的思想中放入一些东西，让他们未来可以用这些素材来进行深入思考。

> Give them something to contemplate:
1. Give them a book to read[^65]  
2. Send them podcast to listen to[^66]  
3. Send them something you saw on Instagram[^67]  
> Just start to put it in their minds. That's maybe something I need to start thinking about here.[^68]  

Dr. Meg Jay 的最后一条建议非常动人：“==请拿出勇气去想象你的生活会进展顺利==。”  
这种想象不是盲目乐观，而是为了让你看清：为了达到那个理想的状态，你今天需要做出哪些具体的努力。照顾好当下的每一分钟，岁月的长河自然会给你满意的答卷。  
无论你现在 25 岁、45 岁还是 55 岁，修正航向永远不嫌晚。你依然握着飞机的操纵杆，你依然可以定义你的未来。  
> Starting from where we are every single day, at every age, the same advice applies.[^69]  

> Have the courage to imagine your life going well.[^70]
