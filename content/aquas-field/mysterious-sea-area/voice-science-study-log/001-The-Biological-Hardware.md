## 📅 Session 1: The Chassis & Power Source
**Textbook**: *Anatomy Physiology for Speech, Language, and Hearing (Seikel et al.)*

### 1. The Language of the Map (Anatomy Terms)
Before understanding the machine, we defined the standard orientation terms used in all medical literature:
*   **Anterior (Ventral)**: Front.
*   **Posterior (Dorsal)**: Back.
*   **Superior (Cranial)**: Top (Head).
*   **Inferior (Caudal)**: Bottom (Tail).
*   **Medial**: Center.
*   **Lateral**: Side.

> **Visual**: The Vocal Folds run *Anterior-Posterior*. They open *Laterally*. They close *Medially*.

### 2. The Power Source (Respiration)
**The Myth**: "Sing from the diaphragm."
**The Reality**: The diaphragm is strictly a muscle of **inhalation** (flattening downward/inferiorly to create space). It essentially relaxes during the exhale (singing).
**The Mechanism**:
1.  **Imbalance**: We inhale (expand). The lungs are stretched.
2.  **Recoil**: Tissue wants to snap back to resting state.
3.  **Support**: We use inspiratory muscles (external intercostals) to *hold back* the recoil, while expiratory muscles (abdominals) *guide* the pressure. It is a dynamic tug-of-war.

### 📝 Session 1 Quiz (With Answers)

#### Q1: Mechanics
**Why is the rib movement described as "Bucket Handle" and "Pump Handle" motion?**

> **Answer:**
> *   **Pump Handle (Sternum/Upper Ribs):** The sternum (breastbone) moves **Anteriorly-Superiorly** (Up and Out) like an old-fashioned water pump handle. This increases the **Anterior-Posterior** (Front-to-Back) dimension of the chest.
> *   **Bucket Handle (Lower Ribs):** The rib cage lifts **Laterally** (Out to the sides) like lifting the handle of a bucket. This increases the **Transverse** (Side-to-Side) dimension of the chest.
>
> *Result:* The chest cavity expands in **three dimensions** (Vertical via diaphragm, Front-Back via pump, Side-Side via bucket), maximizing volume for air.

#### Q2: Physics
**Explain Boyle's Law in the context of the lungs.**

> **Answer:**
> **"Pressure and Volume are inversely proportional."**
> When the diaphragm contracts (lowers) and the ribs expand, the **Volume** of the container (thorax) **Increases**.
> Because the volume got bigger, the **Pressure** inside drops (becomes Negative relative to the outside air).
> Nature hates a vacuum, so air travels from High Pressure (Outside) to Low Pressure (Inside). *We do not "suck" air in; physics pushes it in.*

#### Q3: Anatomy
**The lungs are not glued to the ribs. What is the "Pleural Linkage"?**

> **Answer:**
> The lungs are wrapped in a membrane (Visceral Pleura) and the rib cage is lined with a membrane (Parietal Pleura). Between them is a tiny bit of liquid.
> **Surface Tension** keeps them together—just like two wet glass slides are impossible to pull apart but easy to slide against each other.
> *Why this matters:* The lungs have no muscle. If this "fluid glue" seal breaks (Pneumothorax), the lung collapses instantly because it cannot follow the ribs anymore.
